  The rate is inversely proportional to carbon intensity: a source at 2.0 kg CO₂e per 100g of
  protein earns the full 1.00×, and anything above that earns proportionally less. There are no
  bands, so nothing is protected by the company it keeps.

  Footprints: Poore & Nemecek (2018), Science — global medians across ~38,000 farms.
  Product data: Open Food Facts, a community database.

  Cheese is costed at roughly double milk protein: it takes about ten litres of milk per
  kilo of cheese, and it carries the share of the herd's footprint that whey is discounted for.
  Applying the allocation at one end only would flatter both.

  Whey is costed by economic allocation: it is a by-product of cheese making, so the
  herd's emissions are apportioned by the value of what the process yields, and whey carries
  a fraction rather than a full dairy share. Under mass-balance accounting instead — dividing
  emissions evenly across everything the herd produces — whey lands nearer 8–9, close to milk
  protein. Both methods appear in published work. This app uses the former, consistently, for
  both the figure and the tier.
